## What Burner cannot catch (honest limits)

- **Code that never runs during the scan**: payloads gated on a real user action we don't simulate (e.g. `git commit` hooks, a specific CLI flag, a date more than 45 days out, a geo/IP check that wants a real public IP). Burner only judges what executes.
- **Sandbox killers**: a payload that first exhausts memory/processes kills the sandbox before it acts. Burner reports this (SANDBOX_DIED → SUSPICIOUS, never clean) but cannot say what would have been stolen.
- **Second stages**: the fake internet never serves real payloads, so for download-and-run loaders Burner sees the download+execute attempt (REMOTE_CODE), not what stage 2 would do.
- **Custom encryption**: exfil under real crypto (AES with an embedded key) can't be matched to a honeytoken; the verdict then rests on 'read your keys + contacted an unknown server'. Base64/hex/base32/url/gzip/zip and repeating-key XOR are decoded.
- **Sandbox-aware malware that stops**: m11 checks for VM artefacts and proceeds anyway; one that exits cleanly when it detects gVisor/Docker would look clean (we log SANDBOX_CHECK as a low-severity hint).
